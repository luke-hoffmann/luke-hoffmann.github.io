I am in a club called: "Design Build Fly." The goal is to design a remote controlled aircraft, build it, and then compete it in an international competition. Everything is done from scratch and here is an overview:

- The flight sciences team utilizes software to generate a stable lightweight aircraft design. They use different techniques to model how the aircraft will behave.
- The structures team (with I am apart of) takes the "net shape" aircraft design, and creates the structures required to achieve a rigid airframe, while staying light enough.
- The competition involves different events where our aircraft is tested. The aircraft has to be able to carry a payload (2L soda bottles).

Part of the building process is to test the "net shape" of the aircraft.
- Building the whole airplane out of balsa wood with the final design isnt efficient when its a test
- We use a computer controlled hot wire foam cutter to cut the shape of the aircraft out of foam. This is used for aerodynamic testing
- The foam cutter was in need of help, so I created a platform for it to sit on. Its a very simple design, but here it is:
  - I used 4 - 2x4s to create a structure
  - I used half lap joints in to connect the 2x4s in a flat configuration
  - I suggested that we add leveling feet so that we can make sure the foam cutter is level, so its accurate.
    - A nut is pressed into some plywood, so its help captive
    - Then a bolt is screwed into the nut and can be screwed in or out to level the structure
  - Pictures will be added in the future, still a WIP
- Once the outside shape of the aircraft is tested, we can start to design how the parts are actually made of balsa wood
- It should be a fun club!
