Every year at highschools across the United States students entering their senior year get to customize their parking spots. Seniors specifically are allocated a spot where they will park their vehicles for the rest of the school year. Then, before the school year starts, seniors come together to personalize their parking spot. Some students spend many weeks planning what they will make their spot look like.

I spent some time in a design software, putting my ideas for my spot into an actual mockup. The premise of the parking spot was to create a visualize of the sun setting over a mountain range. See my first rendition below.

This visualization was initially done for the following reasons:

  • To help visualize what I wanted my parking spot to look like
  • To help me create a stencil to accurately transfer my design to the pavement of the parking spot
  • To pick colors for the parking spot

However, using this visualization to create stencils did not work out.

Stencil Printing Issues

I came up against a few issues when I tried to print the stencil that I had designed on my computer. Since the parking spot was quite large I knew that a single sheet of 8.5" x 11" paper would not be sufficient as a template for the whole space. My plan was to print my design out, cut the design in the appropriate places, and tape together.

However, when I went to print my design, I experienced my printer being inaccurate. For one, the printer did not print to 100% size accuracy. For example, on my computer, the width of a mountain was set to be 4 inches width. The output from my printer was 3.75 inches in width. If I was to print a different sheet, the mount was now 3.8. So, I could not reliably connect different stencils together. This meant that using print outs would not work.

Pivoting

Realizing I had to find a new way to create my mountains, I decided to go with the second option: drawing my stencils by hand, and then cutting them out. I used a very long role of paper, that was intended for covering flooring. I created my stencils and went to paint my spot.

I decided to use spray paint to add color to my parking spot for a few reasons.

  1. Its Cheap - Spray paint can be bought at a low price, perfect for a high school student's budget
  2. Its easy - Spray paint can easily be used with stencils, to get accurate, clean lines
  3. Its fast - I was able to lay a coat of paint in about 2 minutes, and if I lightly dusted the paint, then it would dry quickly

The third point was especially important for my plans to use templates. With traditional roll on, or brush on paint, I likely would have needed to wait longer for drying. With spray paint, I could spray the "color zone" that I needed to, then cover that with a stencil, and move to the next color zone. This ensured that, in theory, no overspray was left (some paint did end up going under the stencils, leaving overspray).
