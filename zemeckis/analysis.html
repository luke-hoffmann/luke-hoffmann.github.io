Essential Questions

1. How do iconic film directors critique the Zeitgeist from which they come?

2. How do directors create a desired effect (especially tone or theme) through their cinematic choices?

How do iconic film directors critique the Zeitgeist from which they come?

Film directors critique the Zeitgeist from which they come by placing viewers in the shoes of characters who experience the negative aspects of their time. For example, in Forrest Gump, Zemeckis uses Forrest to help viewers understand the nature of the era. Forrest travels through all walks of life, and his naive, ambivalent attitude allows the viewer to grasp the chaos that defined the 1950s to the 1980s. Forrest serves as a tool for Zemeckis to critique the Zeitgeist of his origin.

Additionally, in Back to the Future, Zemeckis again places the viewer inside a character’s perspective. Marty McFly is used to show how nostalgic love for earlier decades (the 1950s) is often misguided. Zemeckis reveals that society’s perception of both its own Zeitgeist and past Zeitgeists is flawed. When Marty goes back in time, he sees bullying, conformity, and imperfections. Even the setting contributes to this critique. In the 1950s, the town appears perfect and uniform, whereas Marty’s original time is more diverse and sometimes chaotic.

How do directors create a desired effect (especially tone or theme) through their cinematic choices?

Directors use all techniques available to create their desired effect. Specifically, Robert Zemeckis is a strong proponent of visual effects. Throughout his films, he pushes the boundaries of what VFX can do while maintaining strong storytelling. In his movies, VFX is not the headliner. Transformers, for example, overuses VFX, but Zemeckis avoids this. He uses VFX to support the narrative. VFX contributes to tone by allowing cost-efficient, expressive visuals. This enables him to allocate budget to other cinematic elements. His use of VFX allows for more grandiose visual storytelling. In Contact, the massive machine or tunnel sequences convey the characters' psychological states through scale.

Zemeckis achieves his desired effect—usually narrative immersion—by making fantasy plausible through VFX. Many of his stories cannot be executed with practical effects. A plane flying upside down, the entirety of The Polar Express, or Forrest Gump scenes where Forrest meets famous figures are only feasible through VFX. Thus, Zemeckis’s choice to prioritize VFX enables him to tell stories otherwise impossible to realize.
